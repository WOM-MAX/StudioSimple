### Official learning objective

Demonstrate understanding of general ideas and explicit information in simple adapted and authentic written or digital texts about varied topics.

### Class objective

**Identify the main idea of Anne Frank’s diary entry and use explicit details to describe the order of events in her birthday.**

**Observable outcome:** The student selects a main idea supported by the whole passage, locates details such as dates, times, people, presents and school events, and places key events in order.

### Student language

Visible student-facing language, questions and response models are in English. Mentor guidance and optional support are in Spanish. The original reading on the textbook page remains the primary resource.

## 1. Step 1 — Mentor preparation

**Purpose:** Help the student read for the whole idea first, then return to the text for details.

**Say:** “First, read to understand what the whole diary entry is about. Then use details to answer questions.”

**Route:** Predict → read → identify the main idea → find details → sequence events → answer.

**Mentor guidance:**

1. Open the original textbook page 18 before asking questions.
2. Distinguish the date of the diary entry from the date of the birthday described.
3. Accept an answer in English or Spanish during support; invite a short English response after the idea is clear.
4. The passage says Anne chose “the game” in the sports lesson; the specific game is not identified in this reading.

**Emotional climate:** The reading is a first-person account of a birthday. The student may discuss a preferred present or celebration without being asked to share personal or private information.

## 3. Step 2B — Opening situation and prior knowledge

### Before reading

**Resource:** Show the original Student’s Book page 18, including the heading, pre-reading prompt and diary entry.

**SAY:** “Look at the heading and the first lines. What kind of text do you expect to read?”

**Expected responses:** A diary, a personal account, or a text about an experience. Accept predictions as predictions.

**Hint:** “Which words or page features helped you decide?”

### Starting-point check

**SAY:** “Read the heading carefully. What date is written above the diary entry?”

**Expected answer:** Saturday, 13 June 1942.

**SAY:** “Now read the first sentence. Which day is Anne describing as her birthday?”

**Expected answer:** Friday, 12 June.

**Success criterion:** Distinguishes the date written above the entry from the birthday event described in the first sentence.

**Feedback:** “Good reading. The heading dates the diary entry; the first sentence tells us when the birthday took place.”

**Support:** Reopen the top of the original page and underline the heading and the first sentence in different colors.

**No response:** “Let’s read the heading and the opening sentence together. Point to each date.”

**Emotional tip:** Treat predictions as hypotheses. The student may discuss the text without sharing details from their own life.

## 5. Step 4 — Guided conversation

### Question 1 · Main idea

- **Context:** Whole diary entry, printed page 18.
- **SAY:** “What is Anne’s diary entry mainly about?”
- **Expected answer:** Anne describes her birthday: the morning, her presents and the celebration at school.
- **Mentor reveal:** The main idea covers the whole entry: Anne’s birthday and events at home and school.
- **Success:** The answer covers the whole passage, not one isolated detail.
- **Hint:** “Which answer includes both home and school?”
- **Student reveal:** “The diary entry is mainly about Anne’s birthday and what happened during the day.”

### Question 2 · Explicit information

- **Context:** First paragraph of the entry.
- **SAY:** “What time did Anne wake up, and when was she allowed to get up?”
- **Expected answer:** She woke up at six o’clock and waited until quarter to seven.
- **Mentor reveal:** The first sentence says she woke at six; the following sentence says she waited until 6:45 to get up.
- **Success:** Both times are accurate and in order.
- **Hint:** “Find the numbers in the first two sentences.”
- **Student reveal:** “She woke up at six and got up at quarter to seven.”

### Question 3 · Birthday sequence

- **Context:** Whole passage.
- **SAY:** “What happened at school after Anne shared cakes?”
- **Expected answer:** She was allowed to choose the game for the sports lesson; afterwards, her friends danced around her and sang “Happy Birthday.”
- **Mentor reveal:** Preserve the order in the passage and do not name a sport that the reading does not identify.
- **Success:** Includes the next school event and what happened afterwards.
- **Hint:** “Look for ‘At school’ and ‘Afterwards’.”
- **Student reveal:** “She chose the game for the sports lesson. Afterwards, her friends danced and sang.”

### Question 4 · Opinion with text evidence

- **Context:** Anne’s comment about the diary being her nicest present, printed page 18.
- **SAY:** “Do you agree with Anne that the diary was her nicest present? Say why and support your opinion with one detail from the entry.”
- **Expected answer:** Open response. Accept agreement or disagreement if the student gives a relevant, accurate detail from the passage.
- **Success:** States a position and supports it with a detail from the diary; no single personal opinion is treated as correct.
- **Hint:** “Start with ‘I agree/disagree because…’ and point to a sentence about a present or event.”
- **Mentor reveal:** Evaluate whether the position is clear and the supporting detail is present in the text, not whether the student agrees with Anne.
- **Student reveal:** “I agree because Anne calls the diary her nicest present. Another reader might choose a different present, but should support that view with a detail.”

## 6. Step 5 — Formalization: main idea and explicit details

**Concept:** The main idea summarizes what the whole text is mostly about. Explicit information is stated directly and can be located in the text.

**Key idea:** Identify the main idea from the whole passage; use explicit details as evidence and sequence words to explain order.

**Reading method:**

1. Read the full passage once.
2. Ask what connects most of its details.
3. State the main idea in one sentence.
4. Return to the passage to locate names, times, objects and actions.
5. Use sequence words to describe order.

**Key vocabulary:**

| Expression | Meaning in this passage |
|---|---|
| woke up | stopped sleeping |
| quarter to seven | 6:45 |
| went down to the dining room | went to the dining room downstairs |
| presents | gifts |
| shared out cakes | gave cakes to other people |
| afterwards | after that |

**Model answer:** “Anne’s diary entry is about her birthday. She woke up early, received presents and celebrated at school.”

**SAY:** “Which details prove that this sentence matches the whole passage?”

**DO:** Write one sentence for the main idea and underline one supporting detail from the home section and one from the school section.

## 7. Step 5B — Check understanding

### Check 1

**Question:** “Is ‘Anne received a blue blouse’ the main idea or an explicit detail?”

**Expected answer:** An explicit detail; the main idea includes the whole birthday account.

### Check 2

**Question:** “Which happened first: Anne opened her presents or her friends sang at school?”

**Expected answer:** She opened her presents first; the singing happened later at school.

**Support:** Reopen the original page and locate the transition from the sitting room to school.

## 8. Step 6 — Joint practice

### Case 1 · Put the morning in order

- **Context:** First half of the diary entry, printed page 18.
- **Data:** Anne woke at six; waited until quarter to seven; went to the dining room; went to her parents at seven and then to the sitting room for presents.
- **Question:** “Write four events in order using first, next, then and after that.”
- **Procedure:** Underline the time words; number the events; check each event on the page.
- **Reference answer:** First, Anne woke up at six. Next, she waited until quarter to seven and went down to the dining room. Then, at seven, she went to her parents. After that, she went to the sitting room for her presents.
- **Success criterion:** Events are in source order and the times are accurate.
- **Support:** Use the four timeline cards.

### Case 2 · Main idea and evidence

- **Context:** Whole diary entry.
- **Data:** Birthday date, presents at home, cakes and events at school.
- **Question:** “Choose or write the main idea and add two details that support it.”
- **Procedure:** Circle the idea that covers the whole passage; underline one home detail and one school detail.
- **Reference answer:** “Anne writes about her birthday and the events of the day.” Evidence can include receiving her diary and flowers at home and celebrating with friends at school.
- **Success criterion:** The idea covers the passage; both details are stated in the reading.
- **Support:** Ask, “Does this answer explain only one present, or the whole entry?”

## 9. Step 7 — Miniquiz

### Item 1

**What is Anne’s diary entry mainly about?**

- A. Anne’s birthday and what happened at home and school. **Correct**
- B. How to care for a cat every day.
- C. A sports lesson with one named sport.
- D. How Anne travelled to school.

**Corrective feedback:** The main idea must cover the whole entry. Check for both home and school details.

### Item 2

**When did Anne wake up, and when could she get up?**

- A. She woke up at seven and got up at eight.
- B. She woke up at six and waited until quarter to seven to get up. **Correct**
- C. She woke up at quarter to seven and got up at six.
- D. She woke up after school and went downstairs at seven.

**Corrective feedback:** Return to the first sentences and read the times in order.

### Item 3

**What happened after Anne shared cakes with her friends at school?**

- A. Her parents gave her a diary.
- B. Moortje welcomed her in the dining room.
- C. She chose the game for the sports lesson; afterwards her friends danced and sang. **Correct**
- D. She went to bed at six o’clock.

**Corrective feedback:** Find “At school” and then “Afterwards” in the passage.

### Recovery by item

- **Item 1 support:** Sort three details into **at home** and **at school**, then choose the sentence that includes both.
- **Item 2 support:** Rebuild the time line with 6:00, 6:45 and 7:00 from the first paragraph.
- **Item 3 support:** Highlight “At school” and “Afterwards”; arrange the school events in the order stated.

## 10. Step 8 — Summary and closing

**Key ideas:**

1. **Main idea:** read the whole entry to identify what it is about.
2. **Explicit detail:** return to the passage to locate information stated directly.
3. **Sequence:** use time words to explain when events happened.
4. **Two dates:** keep the heading date separate from the date of the birthday described.

**Synthesis question:** “Which two details show that Anne’s entry describes both home and school?”

**Metacognition:** “What helped you most: reading the whole text, locating time words or building a timeline?”

**Celebration:** “Great reading! You used the whole passage and its details to explain Anne’s day.”

**Next-class bridge:** “In the next class, you will express an opinion about a text and support it with evidence.”
