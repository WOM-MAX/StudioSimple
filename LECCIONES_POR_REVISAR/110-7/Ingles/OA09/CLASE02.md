## 6. Explanation and model

**Concepto:** An opinion states what a reader thinks. Evidence is information from the text. A supported opinion connects a clear position with an accurate detail. Different positions can be valid.

**The opinion:** Begin with a clear position. You may agree or disagree with Anne about her nicest present.

**The text detail:** The reading says Anne received a diary and called it her nicest present. This is evidence from the text.

**The reason:** Use because to explain your choice. Make sure your reason connects with a detail that appears in the reading.

**Another valid position:** A reader may prefer the blue blouse. The reading mentions it, so it can support a different opinion.

**The boundary:** Do not add a named sport. A supported opinion keeps details accurate even when readers make different choices.

**Modelo explicado:** I agree because Anne calls the diary her nicest present. I think a diary is useful for recording experiences. The first sentence uses a detail from the reading. The second sentence explains the reader's reason.

**Cómo se construye el modelo, mostrar durante la enseñanza:** 1) Leer la pregunta y precisar qué pide. 2) Localizar en el caso: «Anne calls the diary her nicest present.». 3) Explicar la relación: The first sentence uses a detail from the reading. The second sentence explains the reader's reason. 4) Formular la respuesta: I agree because Anne calls the diary her nicest present. I think a diary is useful for recording experiences. Comprobar que cada afirmación conserva los datos del recurso.

**Error frecuente:** A preference can differ, but its supporting detail must appear in the text.

**Comprobación inmediata:** How can another reader disagree with support?

**Modelo de comprobación:** I disagree because Anne also received a blue blouse. I would prefer a present I could wear.
