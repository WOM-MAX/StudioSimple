## 6. Explanation and model

**Concepto:** To compare texts, identify each main idea and use details from both. Shared words do not mean identical events. A supported opinion should clearly show which text provides its evidence.

**Message A:** Message A describes a museum visit. The school gate is the meeting point at nine, not the museum's address.

**Message B:** Message B describes a reading club at four in the library. Its purpose differs from the museum visit.

**A shared feature:** Both messages invite Sara and include preparation details. This is a similarity supported by both texts.

**A difference:** The museum meeting is at nine with a notebook. The club is at four with a short story.

**The evidence:** A comparison becomes clearer when it includes information from both texts and identifies where each detail comes from.

**Modelo explicado:** Both invite Sara to learning activities. They differ in the activity, time and place. Each message names its own learning activity and its meeting details.

**Cómo se construye el modelo, mostrar durante la enseñanza:** 1) Leer la pregunta y precisar qué pide. 2) Localizar en el caso: «A: museum visit, Friday morning, school gate at nine. B: reading club, Friday afternoon, library at four.». 3) Explicar la relación: Each message names its own learning activity and its meeting details. 4) Formular la respuesta: Both invite Sara to learning activities. They differ in the activity, time and place. Comprobar que cada afirmación conserva los datos del recurso.

**Error frecuente:** Moving a detail from one message to the other changes the meaning of the reading.

**Comprobación inmediata:** Which material belongs to each activity, and what opinion can you support?

**Modelo de comprobación:** The notebook belongs to the museum visit, and the short story to the club. I agree that a notebook can help because A asks students to record observations.
